Day-to-day care

Maintaining Your Unit

Garbage Disposals

Food scraps, bones, popcorn, peels, and other hard items should never go into a disposal. Only soft, liquid, and/or biodegradable foods should be inserted.

Clear drains are key to a well-functioning home. Avoid putting grease, coffee grounds, and hair down sinks and showers. Simple habits like routine hot-water rinses help prevent buildup and extend the life of your plumbing.

Fridges & Freezers

Refrigerators and freezers must not be overfilled. Overstuffing causes food spoilage and can lead to unit failure. Leave space toward the back of the freezer for air circulation.

Door Locks & Codes

When the latch on the inside of your bedroom door is vertical, the door stays unlocked. When the latch is horizontal, the door stays locked and you need your code to open it. Entering your code incorrectly 3 times temporarily locks you out.

Doors are fire-resistant and may shut heavily. Please help them close gently so you don't disturb your neighbors.

Smoke Alarms

Smoke alarms chirp when the battery needs to be replaced. You do not need to remove the unit to change the battery; the compartment is on the side of the unit.

Smoke alarms may never be covered or removed. Tampering with a smoke detector will result in a $500 fine.

Shared spaces

Kitchen Use & Care

Protecting Plumbing & Communal Spaces

These kitchens do not have garbage disposals. Drains and toilets are easily clogged by improper use. To prevent plumbing problems:

  • Scrape all food scraps into the garbage.
  • Place most food waste in the trash during meal prep.
  • Never pour grease or oil down the drain.

Communal Kitchen Supplies

Communal kitchens are stocked with a basic set of cooking supplies for resident use:

  • Pots & pans
  • Cooking utensils
  • Toasters
  • Microwaves
  • Instant Pots

Important: Plates, bowls, serving utensils, mugs, and similar items are not provided. Residents are responsible for leaving items in the kitchen. Housing will not replace missing items during the season.

Cleaning & Courtesy

Kitchens are cleaned once per day by third-party staff. Please keep areas tidy and respect the cleaning schedule.

Induction-Friendly Cookware

Works on induction

  • Cast iron
  • Enameled cast iron
  • Stainless steel
  • Magnetic steel

Does not work

  • Copper
  • Aluminum
  • Glass
  • Ceramic

Tip: Other cookware can work too if it has a magnetic bottom. If a magnet sticks to the bottom of the pan, it is good to go.

Induction Cooktop Safety

Our kitchens use induction cooktops. They heat up fast and have built-in safety features, so it helps to know what they do:

  • Auto shut-off: the cooktop turns itself off after about 30 minutes of inactivity.
  • Hot surface light: an indicator stays lit while the surface is still hot. Wait until it goes out before wiping or touching.
  • Control lock: prevents settings from being changed by accident.
  • Overheat protection: the cooktop shuts off on its own if it gets too hot.

Never leave cooking unattended, even with these safeguards.

Facilities Notifications

Highway Crossing & Tunnel Safety

During your stay at the Gallatin Gateway Inn, you may need to cross Highway 191 on foot to reach the gas station or post office.

For your safety, there is a pedestrian tunnel just south of the property that goes under the highway. Maps showing two routes from the GGI property to the tunnel are available at the front desk and in the Maps section.

In recent years, staff and community members have expressed concern about residents walking along the highway or crossing directly, especially given traffic, weather, road conditions, and time of day.

The tunnel may not be the most direct route, but we strongly recommend using it. It is the safest way to cross the highway.

Cooking & Fire Alarms

Smoke alarm & cooking safety

All hallway smoke detectors are tied to the building's general alarm system. This means:

  • If a hallway detector is triggered, all alarms in the building, including bedrooms, will sound.
  • The system automatically notifies the local volunteer fire department, who must respond immediately.

False alarms cause major disruption for residents and responders alike.

Cooking safety guidelines

  • Exercise caution when cooking in apartments and communal kitchens.
  • Avoid high-heat cooking with fatty meats or oils.
  • Do not use cooking methods that produce smoke.
  • Always pay close attention to food while preparing it.

Note: Residents may be fined if unsafe cooking triggers a false alarm that dispatches the fire department.

Gallatin Gateway Inn Parking

If you have a vehicle in the parking lot, please register it in StarRez so it is not ticketed, towed, or booted.

Register your vehicle

When a registered vehicle can still be ticketed or towed

Registered resident vehicles will not be ticketed or towed unless the vehicle:

  • Is parked in a space reserved for disability parking only
  • Is parked in a no parking zone
  • Is parked in a fire lane
  • Is blocking access to the trash receptacles or dumpsters
  • Is clearly disabled and has broken parts, glass, or flat tires

Penalties

  • First offense: courtesy notice
  • Second offense and thereafter: $25.00

Any repeat offense, more than three (3) ticketed offenses in any one week, or more than ten (10) ticketed offenses in any consecutive eight (8) week period, will result in immediate disablement of the vehicle by booting or other appropriate means and/or towing and impoundment. The fine for release of any booted or impounded vehicle is $500.00 per offense. The property manager may also determine other special purposes from time to time.

All offenses are evidenced by a ticket issued by an authorized Property Manager. The fine is due and payable, in cash, at the office of Property Management within five (5) days after the ticket is issued. A record of all offenses, including the name and address of the offender, the license plate number of the vehicle involved, sanctions levied, and fines paid, is kept and maintained by Lone Mountain Property Management.
